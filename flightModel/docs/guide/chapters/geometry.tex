Format & ASCII STL & Only ASCII STL keeps the solid names, which become the patches. \\
Solid names & \val{nosecone}, \val{body}, \val{boattail}, \val{fins} & The dictionaries, the boundary conditions and the force integration use the patch names. \file{Allmesh} renames the first three to \val{cone}, \val{walls}, \val{tail}. \\

\end{longtable}

A solid with another name does not stop \file{Allmesh}, but the patch then gets no
refinement, no boundary condition and no force integration. Examine the names of a new
file:
\begin{shell}
grep -E '^ *solid ' mesh/stl/<file>.stl
\end{shell}

\section{Design rules of the surface}\label{sec:surface-rules}

\file{mesh/makeRocket.py} applies these rules. Each one prevents a known failure of
the layer extrusion (\cref{sec:layers}).

\begin{longtable}{@{}P{0.30\linewidth} P{0.62\linewidth}@{}}
\caption{Design rules of the surface.}\label{tab:surface-rules}\\
\toprule
Rule & Principle \\
\midrule
\endfirsthead
\toprule
Rule & Principle \\
\midrule
\endhead
Round all external edges of the fins (leading edge, trailing edge, tip) and the base edge. & At a sharp edge the layers extruded from the two adjacent faces meet and tangle. A radius larger than the layer stack lets the layers turn around the edge. \\
A fillet at each fin root. & At a concave corner of approximately $90^\circ$, the layers of the fin and of the body converge to one node. The fillet replaces the corner with a curve that the two layer stacks can follow. \\
Fins and body in one shell; the fin root cut into the body. & With separate shells the junction is not an edge of the surface, and the layers of the two shells meet at one node. \\
Nose and body as two closed shells on coplanar caps; the shoulder rounded. & A sharp shoulder in one continuous hull gives negative cell volumes. The rounded shoulder lets the boattail stay in the body shell, which the fillet of the trailing edge needs. \\
Facets around the axis: as few as give a smooth surface. & More facets give more small faces where the shells meet, and more bad cells. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

The radius of an edge is set in the section parallel to the axis. Normal to a swept
edge it is smaller by the cosine of the sweep angle.

\section{Write the geometry}\label{sec:makerocket}

\begin{shell}
python3 mesh/makeRocket.py mesh/stl/<name>.stl [--finRoll <deg>] [--dims <file>] [options]
\end{shell}

The script writes the STL file and its two edge files. It writes them only if the
surface is closed and all normals point out. \cmd{python3 mesh/makeRocket.py -h} gives
the dimensions and their defaults; \ent{--dims} reads them from a file with one
\texttt{name value} on each line.

For a half mesh, turn the fins so that the plane $z = 0$ is a plane of symmetry of the
fins (\ent{--finRoll}). The STL file always describes the whole rocket, because the
half mesh is cut from the full mesh (\cref{sec:half-mesh}).

\section{Use a new geometry}

\begin{steps}
\item Write the STL file and its edge files with \file{makeRocket.py}, or put them in
  \file{mesh/stl/}.
\item Examine the file against \cref{tab:stl-req,tab:surface-rules}.
\item Write the reference values and the fin data of the new rocket in
  \file{mesh/meshInfo} (\cref{sec:meshinfo}). The refinement regions scale with
  \ent{lBody} and \ent{rBody}.
\item Examine the domain limits in \file{Allmesh} against \cref{sec:domain}.
\item Make a new mesh. Remove the meshes of the old geometry: \file{runMesh.sh} does
  not compare the geometry.
\end{steps}
