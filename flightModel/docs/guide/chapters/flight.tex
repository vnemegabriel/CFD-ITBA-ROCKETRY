\chapter{Flight data}\label{ch:flight}

\file{flightData/cdFlight.py} uses the telemetry of a flight computer to calculate the
atmosphere of the day, the speed, the drag coefficient of the coast phase and the flight
conditions of a study. It plots the flight $C_D$ against the CFD runs and, optionally,
an OpenRocket simulation.

\section{Command}

\begin{shell}
python3 flightData/cdFlight.py [--csv <telemetry>] [--mass <kg>] [--runs <runs-dir>]
        [--ork <file.ork>] [--alphas <list>] [--tanchor <s>] [--out <png>]
\end{shell}

\file{python3 flightData/cdFlight.py -h} gives the defaults. The mass is the mass after
burnout: $C_D$ is proportional to it.

\begin{steps}
\item \textbf{Atmosphere.} The ground pressure $p_0$ is the mean before launch. During
  the descent the barometer sees no shocks, and the GPS gives the height above the pad
  from the slant range and the horizontal distance. A fit of
  \[
    p = p_0\left(1 - \frac{L\,h}{T_0}\right)^{g/(R L)}
  \]
  to these pairs gives the temperature at the pad $T_0$ and the lapse rate $L$ of the
  day. Hydrostatic equilibrium is the only assumption.
\item \textbf{Speed.} The speed of the flight computer converts pressure to height with
  the standard atmosphere, and its barometer is behind shocks at high speed. The script
  integrates instead the axial accelerometer along the flight path,
  \[
    \frac{dV}{dt} = a_x - g\cos\theta,
  \]
  with $\theta$ the tilt from the vertical, forward and backward from an anchor time in
  the subsonic coast, where the barometric height with the fitted atmosphere gives the
  speed.
\item \textbf{Height and ambient state.} The integral of $V\cos\theta$ gives the
  height; the fitted atmosphere gives $p$ and $T$ at that height.
\item \textbf{Drag.} With the motor off, the axial accelerometer measures the specific
  force $-D/m$:
  \[
    C_D = \frac{-m\,a_x}{\tfrac12\rho V^2 A_\text{ref}}.
  \]
  The data start when the residual thrust ends.
\item \textbf{Check.} Compare the integrated apogee with the GPS apogee.
\item \textbf{Flight conditions.} For each Mach number of the study, the script prints
  the time, the height, $p_\infty$ and $T_\infty$.
\end{steps}

\section{Angle of attack of the flight}\label{sec:flight-alpha}

A rolling rocket gives two lateral accelerations:

\begin{itemize}
\item a centripetal acceleration of a sensor off the axis, $\omega_\text{roll}^2 r$,
  fixed in the body axes;
\item the aerodynamic normal force, fixed in space, which turns in the body axes at
  the roll frequency.
\end{itemize}

The mean of the lateral acceleration, divided by $\omega_\text{roll}^2$, must give a
constant radius $r$. The oscillation at the roll frequency gives the normal force
$N$, so $C_N = N/(\tfrac12\rho V^2 A_\text{ref})$ and $\alpha \approx C_N/C_{N\alpha}$,
with $C_{N\alpha}$ from the CFD. The sensor noise is in the oscillation, so the result
is an upper limit of $\alpha$.
