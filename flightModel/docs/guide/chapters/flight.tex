\chapter{Flight data}\label{ch:flight}

\file{flightData/cdFlight.py} uses the telemetry of a flight computer to calculate the
atmosphere of the day, the speed, the drag coefficient of the coast phase and the flight
conditions of a study. It plots the flight $C_D$ against the CFD runs and, optionally,
an OpenRocket simulation.

\section{Command}

\begin{shell}
python3 flightData/cdFlight.py [--csv <telemetry>] [--mass <kg>] [--runs <runs-dir>]
        [--ork <file.ork>] [--alphas <list>] [--tanchor <s>] [--out <png>]
\end{shell}

\file{python3 flightData/cdFlight.py -h} gives the defaults. The mass is the mass after
burnout, read from the OpenRocket simulation in \file{flightData/Aconcagua.ork} or
given with \ent{--mass}: $C_D$ is proportional to it.

\paragraph{Telemetry format.} A CSV file with a comma between fields and a dot as
decimal sign, without thousands separators, with the column names of the TeleMega
export (AltOS): \ent{time}, \ent{state_name}, \ent{pressure}, \ent{speed},
\ent{accel_x}, \ent{accel_y}, \ent{accel_z}, \ent{gyro_roll}, \ent{tilt}, \ent{pad_range}, \ent{pad_dist}, \ent{nsat}. The script
stops on a decimal comma and on a value outside the physical limits of
\ent{LIMITS} in the script, with the line and the column. To use another flight
computer, convert its export to these names and units first. The accelerometer
saturates during the motor burn; the script uses only the coast.

\section{Method}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[height=0.5\textheight]{figures/pdf/flight.pdf}
  \caption{Calculation of the flight drag coefficient, from the telemetry (top) to the result (bottom).}
  \label{fig:flight-method}
\end{figure}

\begin{steps}
\item \textbf{Atmosphere.} The ground pressure $p_0$ is the mean before launch. During
  the descent the barometer sees no shocks, and the GPS gives the height above the pad
  from the slant range and the horizontal distance. A fit of
  \[
    p = p_0\left(1 - \frac{L\,h}{T_0}\right)^{g/(R L)}
  \]
  to these pairs gives the temperature at the pad $T_0$ and the lapse rate $L$ of the
  day. Hydrostatic equilibrium is the only assumption.
\item \textbf{Speed.} The speed of the flight computer converts pressure to height with
  the standard atmosphere, and its barometer is behind shocks at high speed. The script
  integrates instead the axial accelerometer along the flight path,
  \[
    \frac{dV}{dt} = a_x - g\cos\theta,
  \]
  with $\theta$ the tilt from the vertical, forward and backward from an anchor time in
  the subsonic coast, where the barometric height with the fitted atmosphere gives the
  speed.
\item \textbf{Height and ambient state.} The integral of $V\cos\theta$ gives the
  height; the fitted atmosphere gives $p$ and $T$ at that height.
\item \textbf{Drag.} With the motor off, the axial accelerometer measures the specific
  force $-D/m$:
  \[
    C_D = \frac{-m\,a_x}{\tfrac12\rho V^2 A_\text{ref}}.
  \]
  The data start when the residual thrust ends.
\item \textbf{Check.} Compare the integrated apogee with the GPS apogee.
\item \textbf{Flight conditions.} For each Mach number of the study, the script prints
  the time, the height, $p_\infty$ and $T_\infty$.
\end{steps}

\section{Angle of attack of the flight}\label{sec:flight-alpha}

The lateral accelerometers ($a_y$, $a_z$) measure two forces:

\begin{itemize}
\item a centripetal acceleration of a sensor off the axis, $\omega_\text{roll}^2 r$,
  fixed in the body axes;
\item the aerodynamic normal force $N/m$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{steps}
\item Fit $a_y$ and $a_z$ against $\omega_\text{roll}^2$ in the last part of the coast
  (\ent{--rfit}), where $N$ is small. The fit gives the radius $r$ and a bias for
  each axis. The radius must be constant; the flight gives about 2.9~cm.
\item Subtract $\omega_\text{roll}^2 r$ and the bias from the measured acceleration.
  The magnitude of the remainder is $N/m$.
\item Calculate
  \[
    \alpha \approx \frac{m\,\lvert a_\text{lat} - \omega_\text{roll}^2 r - b\rvert}
    {q\,A_\text{ref}\,C_{N\alpha}},
  \]
  with $C_{N\alpha}$ from the CFD (\ent{--cna}). The script plots the median and the
  quartiles of $\alpha$ against the Mach number, on the right axis of the $C_D$ figure.
\end{steps}

The sensor noise is in $\lvert a_\text{lat}\rvert$, so the result is an upper limit of
$\alpha$. With \ent{--rocketpy}, the script also plots the angle of attack of a RocketPy simulation
of the same flight. This is a model with a forecast wind, not a measurement.

The script does not use the tilt of the flight computer to find $\alpha$. The tilt
oscillates at the roll frequency, with an amplitude of $1.7^\circ$ in the subsonic
coast and about $3.5^\circ$ above Mach 1. The lateral force does not show an angle of
attack of this size. The cause is not measured.

\section{Sources of uncertainty}

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}P{0.22\linewidth} L@{}}
\toprule
Source & Effect \\
\midrule
Mass & $C_D \propto m$. A design mass is not a measurement. \\
Wind & The speed is relative to the ground. \\
Flight path & The gravity term uses the attitude as the direction of the path; valid at small $\alpha$. \\
$C_{N\alpha}$ & One value for all Mach numbers, from the CFD at M 0.3 to 0.8; it scales $\alpha$ directly. \\
Atmosphere & One lapse rate for the whole column; real layers deviate from it. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
