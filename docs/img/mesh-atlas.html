01The block structure

The fluid around a pointed body of revolution is topologically an annulus whose hole closes at the nose tip and reopens at the base. A single radial O-grid cannot cover it — carried into the tip, every radial line converges on one point and the cells collapse. That is a property of the shape, not of the mesher, and it is why the topology changes in exactly two places and nowhere else.

Two indices describe every block: a level (radial position in the cross-plane) and a segment (streamwise position). Refinement is always a statement about one of those two directions, or about the azimuthal one that runs through all of them.

N_AZ is by far the most expensive knob, because it is the only one that multiplies the whole mesh. It is also the one you are most likely to reach for, since it sets the surface cell size. Reach for a radial or axial change first.

Under the hood there are exactly four families of transfinite curve, and every cell in the mesh is sized by one of them. Everything in meshParams.py resolves into this table.

Why per-station bias matters

Shell 1's count is the same at every station — it has to be, or the blocks would not conform. Its bias is not. At a wall station it clusters at the wall (coefficient 1.0777, first cell 303 µm). At the inlet plane it is uniform, because there is no wall there to cluster on and a 0.3 mm cell next to a 1.5 m cell is an aspect ratio, not a resolution. Opposite edges of a transfinite face must share a count, never a progression.

04The sizing law

This is the whole reason the file reads differently from a snappyHexMeshDict. You do not specify refinement levels and discover the cell size afterwards. You specify the cell size at each end of a stretch, and the count falls out of two closed-form equations.

hN = h1 · c(n−1)     and     L = h1 (cn − 1)/(c − 1)
⟹   c = (L − h1) / (L − hN)     n = 1 + ln(hN/h1) / ln c

Both are exact. "0.303 mm at the wall growing to 20 mm at r = 0.35 m" is a statement about the mesh, not a wish about it — no iteration, no relaxation, no quality-driven retreat.

One consequence is worth internalising, because it is what makes subdivision safe: in a geometric stack the cell size is exactly linear in distance along, h(s) = h₁ + (c − 1)·s. So splitting a segment and re-solving each piece reproduces the parent distribution rather than approximating it. That is how nose becomes 14 blocks without you having to think about it.

Two errors it will now give you

cell size (1.5, 1.25) is not smaller than the segment it has to fill (1.48 m) — you shrank the domain but left H_UP_INLET at 1.5 m. Shrink it too.

An OverflowError from inside the solver used to be possible when a fine y⁺ with a gentle expansion drove n into the hundreds. That path is now overflow-safe and returns inf, so the bisection converges instead of dying.

Reading that table

Going wall-resolved is nearly free in this topology. y⁺ 32 → 1 costs the same 1.45× as merely halving the near-field cell, because y₁ only sets where the geometric stack starts — the count grows logarithmically, not linearly. There is no layer algorithm to collapse, so the change is exactly the change you asked for.

The cheapest real refinement is streamwise. Cylinder Δx 12 → 8 mm buys a third more axial resolution for 19%, and it lands where the boundary layer is developing.

The domain is the free lunch. Cutting 118 D upstream to 59 D and the farfield to 6 m gives back 8% of the mesh with, at this Reynolds number, no defensible effect on the forces — and it removes the highest-aspect-ratio freestream cells at the same time.

If you shrink the domain, shrink H_UP_INLET

The upstream segment is subdivided into 6 equal pieces. H_UP_INLET = 1.5 m has to fit inside the first of them. At 3 body lengths upstream that piece is 1.48 m and the solver refuses — clearly, naming both numbers. Use 0.6 m.

07Invariants you must not break

Most of these are asserted in code and will stop you. The ones that are not are marked, and those are the ones to hold in your head.

every boundary face in a physical group or gmshToFoam invents defaultFaces checked: 175,396 = exact face count
Counts are shared, biases are not

If you find yourself wanting a different number of radial cells at one station than another, the block structure is telling you it needs another radial split, not a different count. Add a shell. That is what shells are for.

08Traps, and the one open defect

Open — fix this before quoting any number

Non-orthogonality: mean 13.13°, max 85.82°, 63,209 faces above 70°. It sits entirely in the azimuthal faces — axial faces are 0.69° mean with none above 70.

Cause. The annular sector face blends radius linearly in index while the radial distribution is geometric (0.303 mm → 20 mm), multiplied by the 45° arc-to-chord sagitta of 0.0761. In a pure polar grid azimuthal faces are at exactly 0° — confirmed by the validation below.

Fix, designed and costed. Split the azimuthal direction into more blocks. A ring block (square edge → 45° arc) splits cleanly into N pieces; the core stays one block; conformity holds. Sagitta: 45° → 0.0761, 22.5° → 0.0192, 11.25° → 0.00482, a 16× reduction. Set N_AZ = 20 (divisible by 4) — 2,534,880 cells, 1.12× base. Changes needed: the cross_quads() tiling and intermediate azimuthal columns in Station.uv().

The measuring instrument is validated

meshQuality.py computes OpenFOAM's definitions, not gmsh's SICN — different numbers, and a mesh can pass one and fail the other. Checked against meshes with known answers: uniform cube 0.000e+00, graded cube 0.000e+00, polar annulus 0.000°, 45° shear → exactly 45.000°. So the numbers above are real, not an artefact.

09Files and commands

aconcaguaGeom.py
The geometry of record, replacing the STL. Five defining numbers, everything else derived. Self-verifying: reproduces all 2016 body vertices to 2.7e-7 m, which is the STL's own float32 write precision. Also carries the parametric fin sections.
meshParams.py
The only file you normally edit. Run it alone to print the plan before building anything.
blockTools.py
Memoising structured-block layer over gmsh's built-in kernel. Every entity is keyed on its defining data, so neighbouring blocks share edges automatically — which is what makes a 177-block decomposition tractable by hand.
buildHexBody.py
The topology: levels, segments, stations, patches.
meshQuality.py
OpenFOAM's quality measures, validated against known meshes.
meshFinish.py
build → snap → audit → export.
fixPatchTypes.py
Run immediately after gmshToFoam. Not optional.
# plan the mesh before building it
python3 meshParams.py

# build, snap, audit, write the .msh   (~50 s, 334 MB ASCII — generate locally)
